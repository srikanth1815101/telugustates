# Community development

Communities start with a natural landscape. Only reviewed occupied houses count. Their private access and village paths appear before any electrification. A project must have an authorized order, sufficient published currency, a local population meeting its threshold, a reviewed need and a site that passes the frozen terrain and ownership checks. Approved positions are copied into ledger history; gardens and homes are never regenerated to spend resources. A school or health centre cannot be purchased for an empty forest. No project has been funded in this publication.

Village stages: natural, growing, connected and developed. Local wells, paths, bridge decks, lamps, schools and health posts are distinct works. A proposed bridge must also have suitable banks and a registered mapped-water crossing. Proposals without a suitable site are refused. Historical works remain recorded if occupancy later changes.

Mapped area 275,144.43 km². Generated in-state cells 67,989,460; terrain-eligible 61,414,158; connected claimable 17,233,329; locked reservations 300; remaining connected 17,233,029. Community allocations are separate from historic reservations. Eligibility is the project’s virtual terrain and frontage rule, not a legal or cadastral determination of developable land.

# Art direction · field notes

Start at the reservoir. From the shore the water should be brighter than the bank; from the overlook the dark wooded ridge should still be recognizably the same ridge. At a real gate, a person, a banana leaf and a boundary stone should read at their own sizes. The world is not scaled down to look like a toy.

**Shape first.** Use broad angular planes for exposed slopes, boulders, cap rocks and the cool-upland spire. Shared, indexed terrain vertices and a face shader give the close mesh a cut-paper look without requiring three new positions per triangle. Plutonic shapes are clustered, not laid out in even rows. Crops can use rows because that reads as cultivation. Palms fan outward, banyans send roots down, bamboo uses several stems, acacias spread sideways and flowering trees put warm accents on the outer crown. Far woodland is a cheaper stand silhouette. Small flowers are groups, not individually downloaded assets.

**Where smooth works.** Keep sky gradients, haze, shore colours and water reflections continuous. Blended pads by doors protect the human scale and collision. Use soft, terrain-following contact shade under vegetation and stone, and actual local shadow maps only on High. The optional pseudo-random weather is calm; the atlas is not a hunting game.

**Three palettes.** Monsoon tilts green and brings more wetness, summer tilts ochre and dusty green, winter is quieter with a pale misty horizon. Golden hour is the initial light. Day and Dusk are convenient lighting studies at the same map position; Live IST uses the actual clock, location and astronomical direction. Plant and animal placement is **stylized, not a survey**. A dramatic reference cliff is not a reason to put mountains, firs or waterfalls on a flat delta.

**How to judge the match:** compare the eight camera triplets in `debug/reference_match/` with the six supplied references. The review crops are user references, not in-game textures or distributable game art. Check the skyline first, then shapes at the feet, then close-far transitions. Beige/pink stone, sunlit crowns, a readable skyline and a visible household beacon are design goals, not evidence of performance. The world is less densely art-directed than the reference game: lowland skylines are flatter, some mid-distance covers repeat, tiny animals are easy to miss, and a rare encounter cannot be guaranteed by a preset name. The physical Android gate remains independent of the stills.

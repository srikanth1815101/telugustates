# What the rain simulation measures

**Try it:** Open meadow → ground view → scenery → Light rain / Rain / Heavy rain. Stay put about 45–60 seconds. The numbers below describe an accelerated game, not a weather record for Markapuram. Changing settings preserves local water already on the ground.

- **Input and losses.** Each sampled land area gets rainfall volume in cubic metres. A finite soil-uptake rate, evolving saturation, evaporation and a small first film precede running flow. The three manual examples represent about **3.6 / 17.6 / 50.7 mm/h**. During rain, forty storm seconds pass per active play second. Once rain stops, the same solver processes fifteen model seconds per active second; the visual glints move in ordinary scene time. This lets a person watch a shower develop without waiting half an hour.
- **Down a slope.** World-fixed irregular sites about 3 m apart are connected into triangles. Grades and intervening bed samples come from the same submitted terrain used by the boots, with homes and larger rock footprints excluded. A steeper receiver is preferred; enough mobile water can feed a second viable downslope path. A receiving permanent lake/river or the edge of the local model is an open boundary. Water is not generated on the lake and called runoff.
- **Width, not just colour.** An edge carries its share of the local discharge. A shallow triangular section and the Manning relation convert that discharge, slope and bed roughness to depth, width and average speed. Very diffuse flow is represented as damp ground, not another painted channel. Source widths grow from zero, at a join the combined stream broadens downstream, and split branches are sized separately. Overlapping reaches are united in a two-scale ground-space distance field before lighting and rain impact shading. They conform to the original triangles.
- **A hollow is not an outlet.** A depression's area–elevation table turns retained volume into a water level. The lowest connecting saddles carry excess under positive head; the exchange cannot lower the source below the lip or reverse two pools' levels. The receiving ground or lower pool actually gets that volume. Plane/bed intersections, sub-metre shoreline samples and thin-edge attenuation give pools their shapes. Soil and evaporation continue after rainfall stops. Between dry solver updates the level and bed-intersection interpolate from the previous solved head to the new one, including boot contact.
- **Scale and limits.** Shallow field hollows are procedural additions to the walkable inland bed, not mapped potholes. Terraces, the permanent bank and the marine swash zone remain distinct. One contiguous, roughly 210 m window follows the local view. Overlapping ground stores and flows are carried across page boundaries. Recently visited sites retain state in bounded memory; new ground uses antecedent wetness. This is not a statewide flood model, measured bathymetry, or an erosion simulator.

## Dry ground, open water and surf are separate

- Precipitation drives the weather label, droplets, rock wet streaks, impacts and the land-stream display. An automatic shower below the display threshold is dry. Clouds, the month, retained soil colour and a full hollow cannot enable moving land water. Switching off the rain disables the ground shader branch **and** publication of its flow maps on the first dry frame; the balance continues below it.
- A running reach has broad advected glides and narrower shear lanes, interrupted by pockets of capillary waves with their own extent, bearing and wavelength. Only their *local* coordinates turn with the current. Continuous, staggered backtraces carry the surface downstream; marks need not cover the whole river. A small angular survey of the loaded terrain supplies the bank/sky transition for reflection. It is not a live mirror of every tree or house. Sparser leaves and irregular bubble strings follow integrated wet paths. These are not rain effects. Lakes do not acquire a downstream clock; regional survey water is cartography.
- On an active freshwater margin, shallow moving pressure patches raise and uncover small parts of the fitted bed. They are not storm runoff, wave run-up from the sea, lake-level change or a temporary puddle. See [River current and bank contact](River-current.md).
- The beach face retains its near-drying swash law. In a different depth band the bed shortens an incoming crest, while further offshore two oblique trains meet. The near marine mesh resolves those faces; the normal and froth are tied to the raised vertices. A local depth pass prevents a transparent distant wave from showing through a closer one. There is no such pass on the river or reservoir, no additional reflection camera, and no new texture download. The illustrative swells thin out at the limit of resolved geometry. On foot the contact solver samples the same height law.

## Why these rules

- US EPA, [Storm Water Management Model Applications Manual](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPDF.cgi/P100EA7H.PDF?Dockey=P100EA7H.PDF): distinguishes infiltration and depression storage from runoff. SWMM's characteristic subcatchment width is **not** used as the visual width of a rill.
- GRASS GIS, [r.watershed](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.watershed.html): distributing flow among downslope neighbours can capture both concentration and divergence. The game uses a bounded two-receiver version.
- FHWA, [HEC-22, chapter 4, equation 4-38](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/hydraulics/pubs/10009/10009.pdf): relates open-channel discharge to wetted section, hydraulic radius, energy grade and roughness. The illustrative local rill dimensions are not a site calibration.
- Barnes, Callaghan & Wickert (2021), [Fill–Spill–Merge](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/9/105/2021/): retain depressions and route water across connecting sills after filling. The game uses its own local stage–volume and finite overflow calculation, not the paper's full hierarchy algorithm.
